    READ THE ASSUMPTIONS

    A model is a question,
    not a prophecy.

    This lab asks how chosen feedback rules interact. It does not measure intelligence, predict ASI arrival, or prove alignment impossible.

    Read the full theoretical breakdown ↗
    What actually grows?

    A dimensionless capability index K starts at 1. The three rules are dK/dt = r√K, rK, and rK¹·³⁵. Compute enters r through an assumed 0.28 exponent. These are invented scenario equations, not fitted scaling laws. A rate ceiling and a 10¹² index ceiling bound the simulation.

    How is alignment represented?

    Each node has a synthetic stability state. Guardrails and patches provide repair, while growth, compute, and coordination add pressure. Strong guardrails reduce drift in this model because we explicitly assume they do. The index is neither a probability of safety nor a validated measurement.

    What does “multi-agent” mean here?

    Total compute is shared. A diversity factor and communication overhead alter learning, while seeded node sensitivities produce different drift. This is a system-dynamics proxy, not a simulation of intelligent agents.

    What can you learn?

    Compare feedback hypotheses, change one assumption at a time, and export trajectories. Look for sensitivity and bottlenecks. Real evaluations require operational tasks, robust measurements, empirical calibration, and independent oversight.
